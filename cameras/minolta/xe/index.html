Minolta XE

✎ Edit this page

The Minolta XE (marketed as XE-1 and XE-7 in different markets) was an electronically controlled 35mm SLR camera introduced in 1974, developed in collaboration with Leica as the foundation for the Leica R3. This sophisticated camera featured aperture-priority auto exposure with advanced electronic control systems and precise center-weighted metering. The XE incorporated a reliable electronic shutter system with mechanical backup at 1/100 second, ensuring operation even during battery failure. Built with exceptional quality and precision engineering, it used Minolta's SR lens mount and offered comprehensive manual override capabilities. Popular among professional photographers and serious enthusiasts, the XE was praised for its accurate metering, smooth operation, and reliable performance. Its advanced electronic systems and build quality established it as one of Minolta's most respected cameras, appealing to those who demanded professional-level features and precision.

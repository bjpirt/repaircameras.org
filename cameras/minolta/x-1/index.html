Minolta X-1

The Minolta X-1 was a professional 35mm SLR camera introduced in 1972, representing Minolta's flagship model designed to compete with the Nikon F and Canon F-1. Built with exceptional durability and precision, the X-1 featured a robust all-metal construction, interchangeable viewfinders, and a comprehensive system of professional accessories. The camera offered through-the-lens metering with both stopped-down and full-aperture operation, along with a reliable mechanical shutter system ranging from 1 second to 1/2000 second plus bulb. Known for its exceptional build quality and extensive system compatibility, the X-1 was favored by professional photographers and serious enthusiasts who demanded uncompromising reliability and precision. Its modular design and professional features made it particularly appealing to those working in demanding photographic environments where equipment failure was not an option.

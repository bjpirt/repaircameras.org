Nikon F90

✎ Edit this page

The Nikon F90 (N90 in North America), launched in 1992, was an advanced amateur autofocus SLR that brought professional-level features to serious photography enthusiasts. This 35mm camera featured sophisticated 3D Matrix metering, reliable autofocus with predictive focus tracking, and comprehensive exposure modes including program, aperture-priority, shutter-priority, and manual control. The F90's robust construction incorporated a bright viewfinder, built-in motor drive, and full compatibility with AF and AI Nikkor lenses. Notable features included multiple custom functions, extensive exposure controls, depth-of-field preview, and an optional data back for recording shooting information. The camera was designed for advanced amateur photographers who demanded semi-professional capabilities without the cost of flagship models. The F90 successfully bridged the gap between consumer and professional cameras, offering sophisticated metering and autofocus systems that made it popular among serious photography enthusiasts.

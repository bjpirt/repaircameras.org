Olympus XA

The Olympus XA was a groundbreaking ultra-compact 35mm rangefinder camera introduced in 1979, revolutionizing pocket camera design with its innovative clamshell sliding cover. This remarkably small camera featured a sharp 35mm f/2.8 Zuiko lens, precise rangefinder focusing, and aperture-priority automatic exposure in a package smaller than many point-and-shoot cameras. The XA's unique sliding lens cover not only protected the optics but also served as the power switch, demonstrating Olympus's attention to both functionality and design. Its exceptional build quality, reliable electronics, and surprisingly good image quality made it a favorite among photographers seeking the ultimate in portability without sacrificing performance. The XA became an icon of 1980s camera design, proving that professional image quality could be achieved in an incredibly compact form factor that fit easily in a shirt pocket.

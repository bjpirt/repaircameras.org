The Hasselblad 1600F, introduced in 1948, was the Swedish manufacturer's first single-lens reflex camera and marked their entry into professional medium format photography. This groundbreaking 6x6cm camera featured a unique focal plane shutter with speeds up to 1/1600th second, revolutionary for its time. Built with Hasselblad's renowned precision engineering, the 1600F established the modular system concept that would define the brand, allowing interchangeable lenses, viewfinders, and film backs. Despite some early mechanical challenges with the complex shutter mechanism, the 1600F set the foundation for Hasselblad's legendary reputation among professional photographers and laid the groundwork for future models that would become industry standards.

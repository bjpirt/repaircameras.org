The Olympus OM2000 was a budget-oriented 35mm SLR camera introduced in 1997, representing Olympus's final attempt to continue the OM system in an increasingly autofocus-dominated market. Manufactured by Cosina, this simplified manual-focus camera maintained compatibility with the extensive Zuiko lens system while offering basic through-the-lens metering and match-needle exposure indication. The OM2000 featured a mechanical shutter design that operated without batteries, ensuring reliability in all conditions. Despite its simplified construction compared to earlier OM cameras, it retained the compact dimensions and solid feel that characterized the system. Targeted at photography students and enthusiasts seeking an affordable entry into manual-focus photography, the OM2000 provided access to legendary Zuiko optics while keeping the OM system alive into the late 1990s.

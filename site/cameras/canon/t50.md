The Canon T50 is a compact 35mm SLR camera introduced in 1983, designed as an ultra-simplified camera featuring fully programmed automatic exposure. This camera operates exclusively in program mode, automatically selecting both aperture and shutter speed with no manual override options. The T50 includes TTL center-weighted metering and accepts Canon FD lenses, offering motor-driven film advance and rewind. Notable for its streamlined operation and beginner-friendly design, it eliminates exposure controls that might confuse new SLR users. Targeted at first-time SLR buyers and casual photographers seeking SLR image quality without complexity, the T50 prioritized ease of use over creative control, representing Canon's attempt to attract point-and-shoot camera users to the SLR format.

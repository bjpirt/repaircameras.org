The Nikon F50 (N50 in North America), launched in 1994, was a consumer-oriented autofocus SLR that offered advanced features in an affordable, compact package. This 35mm camera featured sophisticated 3D Matrix metering, reliable autofocus with focus tracking, and comprehensive exposure modes including program, aperture-priority, shutter-priority, and manual. The F50's lightweight design incorporated a built-in pop-up flash, motor drive for automatic film advance, and compatibility with AF and AI Nikkor lenses. Notable features included multiple shooting modes, exposure compensation, and a clear viewfinder with focus confirmation. The camera was designed to appeal to photography enthusiasts stepping up from basic point-and-shoot cameras, offering them access to the Nikon lens system and semi-professional features at an entry-level price point, making quality SLR photography more accessible.

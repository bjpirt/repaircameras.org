The Minolta 9000 was a professional-grade 35mm autofocus SLR camera introduced in 1985 alongside the revolutionary 7000, representing Minolta's flagship offering in their new autofocus system. Built with a more robust construction than the 7000, it featured advanced exposure metering, faster autofocus performance, and enhanced durability for professional use. The camera offered comprehensive exposure modes including programmed auto, aperture-priority, shutter-priority, and full manual control, with a shutter speed range from 30 seconds to 1/4000 second. Its sophisticated metering system and reliable autofocus made it particularly appealing to professional photographers and serious enthusiasts who demanded the cutting-edge autofocus technology in a camera built to withstand heavy professional use and deliver consistent, accurate results.
